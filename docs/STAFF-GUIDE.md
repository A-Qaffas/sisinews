# sisi.news staff guide

For journalists, the Editor-in-chief and admins. No technical knowledge needed. The section on managing the team is for admins only.

## Signing in

Go to `/admin` on the site and sign in with the email and password an admin gave you. There is no sign-up page and no "forgot password" email: if you lose your password, ask an admin to set a new one. If you cannot sign in at all, your account may have been switched off; ask an admin.

The control panel is in English. The stories you write are in Arabic, English, or both.

## Roles: who does what

Everyone on the team has one of three roles. An admin sets it on the person's account, and it is shown as a coloured badge in the **Team** list.

| Role | What the person can do |
| --- | --- |
| **Journalist** | Writes stories and sends them for review, but **only in the sections an admin has assigned to them** (see below). A journalist can never publish: every story goes to the Editor-in-chief first. A journalist sees their own stories only. |
| **Editor-in-chief** (in Arabic: رئيس التحرير) | Writes in **every** section. Reviews what journalists submit, then approves and publishes it (now, or at a time you pick), or sends it back with a note. Can edit any story, unpublish it, and look after the site's pages, categories, tags and live broadcasts. |
| **Admin** | Everything the Editor-in-chief can do, and also manages the team: accounts, roles, passwords and the sections of each journalist. |

**Sections for journalists.** A section is a news category such as Egypt or Business. A journalist can start and edit stories only in the sections an admin has chosen for them: the **Category** list on the story form shows no others. A journalist with exactly one section finds it already chosen on a new story.

- **No section yet.** A journalist who has not been given any section cannot start a story. There is no **New article** button, and a yellow note on the dashboard and on the Articles list says so. Ask an admin to assign at least one section.
- **A section was taken away.** The journalist can still open and read their stories in that section, but can no longer edit or submit them. The story page says so (it is titled **View article**), and in the **Articles** list the **Category** column marks the story **Not your section**. The Editor-in-chief can still edit and submit them, and an admin can give the section back.
- **Editor-in-chief and Admin** write in every section, so nothing is assigned to them.

## How a story is organised

One story has two **language versions**: Arabic and English. They share the section, tags, picture and the featured / breaking switches, but each version has its **own headline, text and status**. That means:

- You can publish the Arabic version today and finish the English one next week.
- A story with only one language filled in is fine. Readers of the other language simply do not see it.
- Submitting, approving and unpublishing always apply to **one language at a time**. The buttons at the top of the story say which: for example "Submit for review (Arabic)".

## Writing a story

1. Open **Articles** and choose **New article**. If there is no such button, an admin has not given you a section yet: see [Roles](#roles-who-does-what).
2. Pick the **Arabic** or **English** tab and fill in:
   - **Headline**: required for each language you want to publish.
   - **URL slug**: filled in for you from the headline. Leave it alone unless you have a reason; the story is found by its number, so the slug only decorates the address.
   - **Summary**: one or two sentences shown on cards, in search results and when the story is shared.
   - **Story**: the text. Use the toolbar for bold, italic, underline, links, headings (H2, H3), quotes, lists and a divider. Pictures can be added inside the text. Anything the toolbar does not offer (colours, fonts, scripts) is removed when you save, so pasted text may lose its styling.
   - **Image caption** and **Search engine description**: optional, but fill them in for important stories.
3. On the right, choose the **Category** (journalists are offered only their own sections; if the one you need is missing, ask an admin) and add **Tags** (start typing to search; if a tag does not exist you can create it, with an Arabic name, an English name, or both). Add the **Hero image**: JPG, PNG or WebP, up to 5 MB, landscape works best.
4. Click **Create**. The story is saved as a **draft**. Only you and the Editor-in-chief can see it. Come back to it any time from **Articles**; use **Save changes** after each edit.

Writing Arabic: type straight into the Arabic tab. The text field switches to right-to-left by itself. If one sentence mixes in English words, that is fine.

## Submitting for review

When a version is ready, use **Submit for review** for that language at the top of the story. The text is saved and goes to the Editor-in-chief. While it is in review you cannot edit it; if you spot a mistake, tell the Editor-in-chief. A journalist cannot publish a story, only submit it.

You do not need the Editor-in-chief's approval to keep writing: you can create as many drafts as you like, in any of your sections.

## What the statuses mean

| Status | Meaning | Who can edit the text |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Draft** | Being written. Nobody outside the newsroom can see it. | The author (in a section still assigned to them), the Editor-in-chief, admins |
| **In review** | Sent to the Editor-in-chief, waiting for a decision. | The Editor-in-chief and admins only |
| **Changes requested** | The Editor-in-chief sent it back with a note. The note is shown in red at the top of the story. | The author (in a section still assigned to them), the Editor-in-chief, admins |
| **Published** | Live on the site. | The Editor-in-chief and admins |
| **Scheduled** | Approved with a publish date that is still ahead (a violet badge, "Scheduled for 14 Oct, 09:30"). Readers cannot see it yet; it goes live by itself at that time. See [Scheduling a story](#scheduling-a-story). | The Editor-in-chief and admins |
| **Unpublished** | Taken off the site with **Unpublish**. It is kept, and the Editor-in-chief can reopen it as a draft. | The Editor-in-chief and admins |

Every change of status is recorded: who did it, when, and any note. Open **Status history** on a version to see it.

## Reviewing (Editor-in-chief)

1. Open **Review queue** to see versions waiting for a decision.
2. Open the story and read the version in review. Check facts, names, numbers, spelling, the headline and the summary, and that the picture and category are right.
3. Then either:
   - **Approve & publish** the version. A box asks **Publish at (leave empty to publish now)**: leave it empty to go live immediately, or pick a time to schedule it (see [Scheduling a story](#scheduling-a-story)).
   - **Request changes** and write a note saying exactly what to fix. The journalist sees the note on the story, edits, and submits again.
4. To take a live (or scheduled) version down, use **Unpublish**. To work on an unpublished version again, use **Reopen as draft**.

You can approve straight from the review queue (the tick button on a phone, **Approve & publish** on a computer) without opening the story. The same **Publish at** box appears there.

The Editor-in-chief and admins, when they write a story themselves, can use **Publish now**, which submits and approves in one step. It has the same **Publish at** box. The history still records both steps.

Approving never publishes a language you have not reviewed: Arabic and English are approved separately.

## Scheduling a story

Scheduling happens at the moment you publish. Only the Editor-in-chief and admins can do it, because only they can approve. A journalist cannot publish or schedule a story.

1. Click **Approve & publish** (or **Publish now** on a draft you wrote yourself), for the language you want. A box opens with **Publish at (leave empty to publish now)**.
2. **Leave it empty** and the version goes live immediately.
3. **Or pick a date and time** in the future, in Cairo time, and confirm. The message says "scheduled for ...". The version goes live **by itself** at that moment: nobody has to come back and click anything.

Rules:

- A time that has already passed is refused by the box. A story is never back-dated. The box also refuses a time more than a year ahead.
- At the chosen time the story is readable at once; the home page and section lists catch up within about a minute, and the RSS feed and sitemap within ten minutes.
- Each language is scheduled separately: the Arabic version can go out at 08:00 and the English one at 09:00.
- Until the time comes the story shows a violet **Scheduled** badge ("Scheduled for 14 Oct, 09:30") in the article list and at the top of the language tab, and the history notes "Scheduled for ...". Readers, the front page, search and the feeds do not show it.
- Scheduling is not editing: the Editor-in-chief can still correct the text of a scheduled version.

To change the time or cancel: use **Unpublish** while the time is still ahead (the history notes "Scheduled publication cancelled"), then **Reopen as draft**, and submit and approve again, with a new time or none. A version that is approved again after it was reopened always gets a fresh time: now, or the one you pick.

## Breaking news and featured stories

- **Breaking news**: switch it on in the story details. Optionally set **Breaking until** (Cairo time); when that time passes the story drops off the ticker by itself. Leave it empty and it stays breaking until someone switches it off. The ticker at the top of the site shows only stories that are published in the reader's language, so a breaking story needs its Arabic version published for Arabic readers and its English version published for English readers.
- **Featured on the front page**: puts the story in the featured slots on the home page. The Editor-in-chief normally decides this.

Use breaking news sparingly: a ticker full of old items stops being read.

## Managing the team (admins only)

Admins have a **Team** item in the sidebar, right under the Dashboard. The Editor-in-chief and journalists do not see it, and if they type its address they are told they have no access. Admins create every account: the site has no sign-up page and sends no email, so you tell people their password yourself.

- **New team member**: click **New team member** and fill in the name, the Arabic name (optional, used on Arabic bylines), the email (the person signs in with it), the role and a password. Passwords need at least 10 characters with letters and numbers. The role is a coloured badge in the list: **Journalist** writes in the sections assigned to them, **Editor-in-chief** also reviews and publishes, **Admin** also manages the team.
- **Sections for a journalist**: while the role is **Journalist**, the account page shows **Sections this journalist may write in**. Pick one or more; each is shown with its English and Arabic name. Leave it empty and the journalist cannot start a story, and their row in the list shows a yellow **None yet** in the **Sections** column. Change the choice at any time: it applies at once. For the Editor-in-chief and Admin the picker is hidden, because they write in every section; whatever was chosen stays saved, so switching the person back to Journalist brings the same sections back. Only admins can change sections.
- **Change anything about an account**: click its row. Names, email, role and the **Active** switch are on that page. You cannot change your own role or switch yourself off, so there is always one admin able to sign in.
- **Reset password** (for someone who lost theirs): in the list, open the **⋮** menu on their row and choose **Reset password**; or open their page and use the **Reset password** button. Type the new password twice, then tell the person. Whoever was signed in with the old password is signed out. Your own password is changed under **Profile** in the menu at the top right, which asks for the current one first.
- **Deactivate and activate**: **Deactivate** (⋮ menu or the person's page) means the person can no longer sign in and is locked out at once, even in the middle of a session. Their stories stay exactly as they are and keep their name. **Activate** lets them in again with the same password. Accounts are never deleted, on purpose, so that every story keeps its author.
- **Several at once**: on a computer, tick the rows, open **Bulk actions** and choose **Deactivate selected** or **Activate selected**. Your own account is always skipped.
- **Find someone**: the search box looks in name, Arabic name and email. The filters narrow the list by **Role**, by **Section** (journalists who may write in it), by **Journalists with no section yet** and by **Active**.

The very first admin is created on the server, not in the panel: see `docs/DEPLOYMENT.md`.

## Quick answers

- **My English version is not on the site.** Check its status. It must be **Published**, not Draft, In review, Scheduled for later or Unpublished. Each language is separate.
- **Can I use the panel on my phone?** Yes. The Articles list and the Review queue show the headline, the status and the row buttons; the other columns appear on a wider screen. Open a story to see everything.
- **There is no New article button.** You are a journalist and an admin has not given you a section yet. Ask an admin to assign at least one in **Team**.
- **I cannot edit a story.** It is probably in review or published, or an admin has taken its section away from you. Ask the Editor-in-chief, who can edit it or send it back.
- **I want to remove a mistake from a live story.** Tell the Editor-in-chief. They can correct the text, or unpublish it while it is fixed.
- **Where are the readers' comments?** There are none: the site has no comments and no reader accounts.
- **The broadcast is on the Live page but will not play.** On YouTube or Vimeo, check that the stream is running and that embedding is allowed. On YouTube the video must be public or unlisted, not private; on Vimeo it must be public (a private or unlisted Vimeo link is refused when you paste it).

## Live broadcasts

The Editor-in-chief and admins have a **Broadcasts** item in the **Newsroom** group of the sidebar (the page itself is called **Live broadcasts**). Journalists do not see it. The site does not stream video itself: the video is hosted and streamed by **YouTube or Vimeo**, and sisi.news only shows it on the Live page. So you first start the broadcast on YouTube or Vimeo as usual, and then register it here.

**Which link to paste.** Use the link of the broadcast itself: `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=…`, `https://youtu.be/…`, `https://www.youtube.com/live/…` or `https://vimeo.com/123456789` (or just the video ID). A channel's live page (`youtube.com/@yourchannel/live`) and a Vimeo event link (`vimeo.com/event/…`) are not accepted: open the stream itself and copy its own address. The form tells you which of the two you pasted. On YouTube keep **Allow embedding** switched on for the stream. On Vimeo the video's embed setting must allow sisi.news (or Anywhere). Otherwise the Live page shows the player but it will not play.

**What readers load.** Nothing comes from YouTube or Vimeo until a reader presses play: the page shows our own placeholder. The Privacy policy explains what those services receive after play.

**How long things stay on the Live page.** A scheduled broadcast that nobody puts live drops off **Coming up** six hours after its scheduled time. One without a time stays there until you put it live or delete it. Ended broadcasts are listed under **Recently ended** for seven days, the six most recent, never with a player.

**Register a broadcast**

1. Click **New broadcast**.
2. Paste the **video link** from YouTube or Vimeo (the address from the browser, or the Share button), or just the video ID. Only YouTube and Vimeo links starting with `https://` are accepted; anything else is refused with a message. Under the box the page says what it recognised, for example "Recognised: YouTube video dQw4w9WgXcQ".
3. Choose the **Governorate** the broadcast is about. Leave it on **Egypt-wide** when it is not about one governorate (a national press conference, for example). You can type the name in Arabic or English to find it.
4. Write a **title** in Arabic, in English, or both. One language is enough; a description is optional.
5. Optionally fill in **Scheduled for** (Cairo time) if you already know when it starts. This only announces the time. The broadcast does not start by itself.
6. Click **Create**. The new broadcast shows a **Scheduled** badge: it is registered, but it is not live.

**Go live and End**

- When the stream is running, click **Go live** on its row (or on its page) and confirm. The confirmation names the broadcast, its governorate and the video. The badge turns red, **Live now**, and readers see the video on the Live page straight away. Check on YouTube or Vimeo that the stream is really running before you press it. On the broadcast's page, **Go live** first saves what you changed there, so a link you have just pasted is the one that goes live.
- When the broadcast is over, click **End broadcast** and confirm. It is no longer shown as live. Nothing is deleted.
- If the stream comes back after ending, use **Resume**. Within two hours of ending, the original start time is kept; later, it counts as a new start (so the Live page does not show an old date).
- The menu item shows a red number while something is live, so a broadcast is not left on air by mistake.

**Changing and removing**

- Click a row to change the link, the governorate, the titles or the scheduled time. You can swap the link even while the broadcast is live, for example if YouTube gave you a new address.
- A broadcast cannot be deleted while it is live: end it first. Open the **⋮** menu on its row (or use **Delete** on its page) to delete a scheduled or ended broadcast. This removes it from the site only; the video stays on YouTube or Vimeo.
- Use the **Status** and **Governorate** filters above the list to find a broadcast; the search box looks in both titles.
